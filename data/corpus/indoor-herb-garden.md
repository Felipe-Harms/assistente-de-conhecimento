# Indoor Herb Garden

Growing herbs indoors is a practical way to add fresh flavour to your
cooking year-round. This guide covers the most common culinary herbs.

## Choosing Containers

Each herb needs a pot with drainage holes. Terra cotta breathes well,
while glazed ceramic retains moisture. A 15-20 cm pot is sufficient for
most herbs. Group pots on a tray filled with pebbles to catch water.

## Light Requirements

Most herbs need 6-8 hours of direct sunlight per day. A south-facing
window is ideal. If natural light is insufficient, supplement with a
full-spectrum LED grow light on a 14-hour timer.

## Watering

Water when the top 2 cm of soil feels dry. Overwatering is the most
common cause of indoor herb failure. Ensure the pot drains freely and
empty the saucer after watering.

## Common Herbs

- Basil: loves warmth and moisture; pinch flowers to extend leaf
  production.
- Parsley: prefers cooler temperatures and consistent moisture.
- Mint: spreads aggressively; keep it in its own pot.
- Chives: very hardy; tolerates partial shade.
- Rosemary: prefers drier conditions; do not overwater.

## Harvesting

Snip leaves from the top of the plant, never remove more than one third
at a time. Regular harvesting encourages bushier growth.
