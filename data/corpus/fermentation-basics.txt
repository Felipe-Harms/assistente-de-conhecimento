Home Fermentation Basics

Fermentation is the controlled microbial transformation of food. Common
fermented foods include sauerkraut, kimchi, yogurt, kefir, and sourdough
bread. This guide covers the basics of safe home fermentation.

Equipment You Need

A clean glass jar, a weight to keep food submerged, and a breathable
cover are essential. Mason jars work well. Avoid metal lids that contact
acidic food; use plastic lids or a layer of cloth.

Salt Brine

Most vegetable ferments use a 2% salt brine. For 1 litre of water, add
20 grams of non-iodised salt. The brine inhibits harmful bacteria while
allowing lactobacilli to thrive.

Vegetables to Try

Cabbage is the classic choice for sauerkraut. Carrots, beets, radishes,
and cucumbers also ferment well. Always use fresh, unwashed produce
when possible — the natural lactobacilli on the surface kick-start
fermentation.

Fermentation Time

Most vegetable ferments are ready in 5 to 14 days at room temperature.
Taste daily after day five. When the flavour is tangy and the texture
is pleasing, move the jar to the refrigerator to slow fermentation.

Safety Notes

Discard any ferment that smells rotten, shows pink mold, or has a fuzzy
texture. Botulism is rare in vegetable ferments but possible in
improperly prepared garlic-in-oil or low-acid preserved foods.
